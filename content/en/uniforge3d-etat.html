So far, our two 3D tutorials reconstruct: AnySplat and InfiniSplat rebuild what your photos saw, and nothing more — a side that was never photographed stays a hole. UniForge3D (Peking University, HKUST, October 2026) adds the other half: a generation branch that completes the invisible, while a reconstruction branch preserves the visible, both exchanging features in a shared "anchor-guided space". This is where the field is heading: no more border between scanning and imagining.

What you get out of it. A verified state of play: what is published, what blocks, and — since the house doctrine is to publish nothing we have not executed — the full log of our installation attempt, then what you can do today with AnySplat, InfiniSplat and TRELLIS.

Keep this distinction in mind, it structures the whole field: reconstruction is faithful but hollow where nothing was seen; generation is complete but invents. UniForge3D claims to marry both without the invented eating the observed, thanks to one trick: the generated structure says, for every voxel, which photo documents it best.

2. What is actually published (checked on 24 October 2026)

The xibushu/UniForge3D repository came out under MIT with an inference script, 18 example cases and the weights on Hugging Face — 26 GB, downloaded here in 232 s. Built on TRELLIS.2 (Microsoft) and VGGT (Meta). So yes, it is published, and no, it is not vaporware. But "published" does not mean "installable on a Friday night". Here is what our lab went through, in order:

ObstacleThe reasonOur tested workaround
setup.sh demands condano conda on the machineuv venv --python 3.10 — identical, faster
torch 2.6.0+cu124 pinnedno kernels for Blackwell cards (sm_120)torch 2.14+cu130 instead
CUDA extensions refuse to compilesystem toolkit CUDA 13.4 ≠ cu124, then GCC 16 vs torch 2.6 headerswith torch 2.14, only the hardcoded -std=c++17 blocked → c++20
flash-attn mandatorythe "routed" attention (the core of the paper) calls flash_attn_varlen_func unconditionallybuild from source — no wheel exists for torch 2.14
DINOv3 is a "gated" repoMeta requires accepting its licence on Hugging FaceHF account + hf auth login, or the community mirror the config file points to

A method lesson, valid for any fresh research code: read the setup.sh before running it, replace what is dated without touching the model's maths, and expect every compiled dependency to fail once, each for a different reason.

3. Licences, because "on Hugging Face" ≠ "free"

The code is MIT. The VGGT-derived weights remain CC BY-NC 4.0 (no commercial use), DINOv3 is gated by Meta, and the default background remover, BRIA RMBG-2.0, requires separate authorisation for commercial use. For a homelab and learning: no problem. For a product: re-read every layer.
